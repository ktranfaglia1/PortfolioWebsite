## Overview

How much do player ratings and opening choices actually predict who wins a chess game? I
cleaned and analyzed more than six million games from Lichess.org to find out.

## Approach

- Loaded and cleaned 6.25 million Lichess games from July 2016 with pandas. The average
  player was rated about 1750.
- Engineered features: five skill groups (Beginner up to Master), the average Elo per game,
  and opening names grouped across their variations.
- Mapped opening preferences across Elo groups and event types, from bullet to correspondence.
- Built logistic regression and random forest models (scikit-learn) to test how well Elo and
  game context predict results.

## Findings

- **Openings follow skill and format.** The Sicilian Defense was the most popular opening in
  every group except Beginners, who favored the King's Pawn. The English Opening and Caro-Kann
  stood out among Experts and Masters. Formats had their own favorites too, such as the Modern
  Defense in bullet and the Italian Game in classical tournaments.
- Elo difference was by far the strongest predictor of who wins. White's win rate climbs from
  50% at even ratings to 93% with a 500-point edge, closely tracking the Elo formula.
- A logistic regression on Elo features alone reached 64.9% accuracy, against a 50% baseline.
- Adding openings, event type, and time control in a random forest did not help: it scored
  60.2% (cross-validated). Those features added noise rather than signal.

Better predictions would need context the game record doesn't capture, like recent form,
move quality, and time pressure.
