## Overview

Celite is an educational toolset for exploring how simple rules produce complex behavior.
Our client, a mathematics professor whose work focuses on discrete mathematical systems,
wanted a way to experiment with and demonstrate those systems through simulation, and to
support the development of their book.

We built three browser-based cellular automaton simulators, with no framework:

- **Conway's Game of Life**, where simple birth and survival rules produce complex patterns.
- **Parallel Wolfram's Nearest Neighbor**, which updates every cell at once.
- **Sequential Wolfram's Nearest Neighbor**, which updates cells one at a time, so the two
  side by side show how synchronous and sequential updates change the outcome.

Each simulator lets users try different rulesets and boundary conditions and watch the
results evolve, making it a hands-on way to study emergent behavior and other ideas in
discrete mathematics.

## Outcome

The client was very satisfied and used Celite in their research. The project was accepted at SUSRC 2025 and is live at [celite.org](https://celite.org), with
full documentation for each simulator.
