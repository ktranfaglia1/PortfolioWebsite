## Overview

Cambridge is a low-lying waterfront city in Dorchester County, Maryland, and is highly
exposed to hurricanes and storm surge. During my internship with the city, I built a desktop
GIS application that turns FEMA's flood-model predictions into interactive views and cost
estimates, designed for local decision-makers rather than GIS specialists.

## What I Built

**Flood simulators.** Four views of the same scenarios, each with controls for projected year
and hurricane category (1–5):

- A 3D elevation model of the county rendered from DEM rasters, with preset camera angles
  and export.
- A 3D surface model from LiDAR data that includes buildings and vegetation.
- A street-level map of Cambridge.
- An interactive Folium map with a flood-risk legend.

**Damage estimator.** For a given property value, it estimates building, contents, and
infrastructure losses under each hurricane category using vulnerability curves, plus
citywide effects such as displacement, debris, and cleanup. Results show as tables and
charts and export to CSV.

**Insurance projections.** Enter an address and home value, and the tool geocodes the address
and projects annual insurance costs up to 100 years out, combining a base rate, inflation,
and rising hurricane frequency and intensity based on NOAA projections.

## Engineering

- Built in Python with PyQt6, using QtWebEngine to embed the web maps in the desktop app.
- Processed elevation data with rasterio (masking and reprojection) and Shapely, with charts
  and 3D surfaces in Matplotlib.
- Loaded large DEM tiles on background threads with on-disk caching, so the app stays
  responsive and each tile loads only once.

## Outcome

The tool gives the city one place to explore flood scenarios and what they would cost, to
support planning for disaster preparedness and mitigation.
