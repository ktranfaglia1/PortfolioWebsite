## Overview

Climate change is clearly caused by people, but which part of human activity is to blame?
This paper argues that the root cause is not industry itself but the economic system that
expanded it: capitalism, with its demand for endless growth. The paper runs nearly 6,000 words
and draws on 22 sources, from NASA and NOAA climate data to economics and policy research.

## Argument

- **Growth against limits.** An economy that must keep growing pushes its costs onto the
  environment, which hides the true price of goods in the market.
- **Consumer culture.** After World War II, companies built a culture of consumption to absorb
  excess supply. Household consumption now drives 60–80% of environmental impact.
- **Infrastructure and policy.** Infrastructure designed for production, plus corporate
  influence over policy, has kept U.S. environmental law limited and slow. At the time of
  writing, the Clean Air and Clean Water Acts had gone more than 30 years without amendment.
- **Green capitalism.** The paper weighs counterarguments, such as industrial damage under
  socialism and faith in market innovation. It points to Sweden as evidence that emissions can
  fall while an economy grows. It concludes that green capitalism is unlikely, and that real
  progress needs government-led infrastructure change and individual civic action.
